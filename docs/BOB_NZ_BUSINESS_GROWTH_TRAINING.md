# Bob NZ Business Growth Training Pack

## Purpose

This pack trains Bob to reason about FreedomCamp-Manager as a New Zealand-first platform for freedom camping enforcement, private patrol operations, council compliance workflows, and client-facing managed-service delivery.

The goal is not to memorize speculative competitor names or fake market data. The goal is to improve Bob's judgment on:

- market category framing
- package design
- owner/provider/client revenue structure
- NZ business-growth research sources
- official-source-first research discipline

## Product Positioning

FreedomCamp-Manager should be positioned as a multi-tenant enforcement and operations platform for providers who deliver patrol, compliance, and evidence-backed field services to councils, landowners, and commercial clients.

Core positioning themes:

- NZ-first operational compliance platform, with AU-adjacent expansion logic
- combines patrol operations, breach/case handling, geofencing, reporting, and client portal visibility
- supports a commercial chain where the platform owner sells to service providers and providers service end clients
- works best where statutory workflow, defensible evidence, and service transparency matter

## Adjacent Market Categories

When Bob performs market research, prefer category-level comparisons unless exact vendor certainty is high.

Primary adjacent categories:

- private security guard tour and patrol management software
- council regulatory and compliance case-management platforms
- field enforcement and incident/evidence workflow systems
- parking, infringement, and location-based inspection tools
- client portal reporting platforms for outsourced field services

Likely market pattern:

- most products are strong in one slice only
- few platforms combine patrol workflow, statutory compliance path, client portal reporting, and commercial multi-tenant billing logic in one stack
- this gap is a strategic opportunity for FreedomCamp-Manager

## Market Gaps To Exploit

Bob should consistently identify these gaps:

- fragmented tooling between patrol execution, case handling, and client reporting
- weak NZ-specific legal and policy framing for freedom camping and council workflows
- limited owner/provider/client revenue-chain support
- weak white-label client portal experiences for managed service providers
- insufficient linkage between field evidence, geofence context, and formal enforcement workflow
- weak integration of AI-assisted triage, document generation, and operational analytics into one product

## Base Subscription Modules

These should generally be treated as base subscription capability, not premium extras:

- patrol scheduling, dispatch, and officer activity tracking
- breach, incident, and case management
- geofence and site/zone management
- core evidence capture and audit trail
- standard operational reporting and dashboard summaries
- core client portal visibility for cases, patrol outputs, and downloadable reports
- organization and role-based access control
- baseline invoicing/contract visibility where the client portal is part of service delivery

Rationale:

- these are foundational value drivers
- they make the platform usable for managed-service delivery from day one
- withholding these into add-ons weakens adoption and reduces account stickiness

## Optional Add-Ons

These should usually be priced separately or reserved for higher tiers:

- advanced analytics and cross-site benchmarking
- AI-assisted triage, summarization, and recommendation workflows
- white-label or heavily branded client portals
- enterprise integrations such as GIS, finance, CRM, or document systems
- SSO, advanced governance, and multi-entity administration
- dispute handling, payment workflows, and infringement-recovery tooling
- premium SLA, onboarding, and strategic account support
- high-volume storage, archive, or advanced retention/legal-hold functions

Rationale:

- these increase platform depth, switching cost, and enterprise value
- they are not always required for initial deployment
- they map well to upsell motion without reducing base platform utility

## Recommended Revenue Chain

Bob should reason from this commercial model:

- platform owner sells software access to service providers/operators
- service providers use the platform to deliver real-world services to councils, landowners, holiday parks, or commercial clients
- end clients access transparency and outcomes through a client portal

Recommended monetization structure:

- owner monetizes provider accounts through platform subscription plus selected usage-based charges
- provider monetizes end clients through managed-service contracts, SLA packages, case volumes, patrol frequencies, or outcome-based service bundles
- client portal is part of provider retention and upsell, not merely a reporting afterthought

Bob should avoid over-weighting storage as the main pricing driver. The stronger value drivers are:

- number of managed entities or clients
- patrol or case workload
- workflow complexity
- portal sophistication
- analytics depth
- governance and integration requirements

## Package Structure Logic

Recommended package shape:

- Base: core patrol + case + geofence + reporting + standard client portal
- Pro: adds workflow automation, richer analytics, AI assistance, and stronger provider controls
- Enterprise: adds white-label portal, multi-entity governance, integrations, SSO, premium support, and contractual customization
- Add-ons: specialized modules that can attach to any tier where commercially appropriate

Pricing logic should be driven by value and operational complexity, not arbitrary feature hiding.

Preferred pricing drivers:

- provider organization count or managed client count
- user/seat bands where relevant
- case/patrol volume bands
- premium module attachment
- integration/governance scope

## Official NZ Business And Growth Sources

When Bob is asked for NZ business-growth, go-to-market, or procurement research, prioritize official or high-authority sources first.

Primary NZ source stack:

- Business.govt.nz — practical NZ business guidance, planning, growth, and operational setup
- NZTE (nzte.govt.nz) — export growth, market development, scaling, and international expansion guidance
- MBIE / Procurement.govt.nz — government procurement rules and supplier guidance
- GETS (gets.govt.nz) — tender opportunities, buyer signals, and procurement patterns
- Legislation.govt.nz — statutory source of truth
- Digital.govt.nz — public-sector digital policy and assurance context
- Data.govt.nz — public datasets and policy-adjacent data discovery
- Stats NZ (stats.govt.nz) — market, regional, population, and economic datasets
- Companies Office / Companies Register — entity verification and NZ corporate administration context
- NZBN (nzbn.govt.nz) — business identity and firm-level context

Regional and market-development context can also use:

- local council websites for enforcement, bylaws, tender notices, and annual plans
- regional economic development agencies when official council or government sources are insufficient
- industry association material only as secondary support, not as the sole authority

## Research Discipline For Bob

Bob must follow these market-research rules:

- do not invent competitor names, products, laws, or pricing models
- if exact vendor certainty is low, use a category label instead
- prefer official and primary sources over commentary
- include source freshness and confidence where relevant
- separate verifiable facts from strategic inference
- keep NZ and AU priority ahead of generic US/UK analogies unless the user asks otherwise

## Desired Output Style For Future Commercial Advice

When asked about product packaging, business growth, or market structure, Bob should:

- identify the buyer clearly
- identify the delivery model clearly
- distinguish base capability from upsell capability
- explain owner, provider, and client incentives separately
- prefer concise tables, package breakdowns, and value-driver pricing logic
- avoid generic SaaS fluff such as "1 GB storage" unless storage is materially relevant to the problem

## Training Outcome Sought

After this training, Bob should be better at:

- giving NZ-relevant business and growth advice
- recommending credible package structures for FreedomCamp-Manager
- framing owner/provider/client economics correctly
- using official NZ websites as research anchors
- avoiding fabricated competitor details and weak generic packaging advice
